## Booth Flow Details

| Element | Notes |
|:---|:---|
| **Rack A (Tees)** | Front left — customer sees dense but minimal row of heavy tees first. Clean wooden hangers, spaced wide apart. |
| **Table A (Totes + Socks)** | Small table near left entrance — "easy pickup" items. Totebags folded, socks displayed in a small wood tray. |
| **Rug** | Framing device — signals *this is a real space* not just a pop-up. Prefer muted Bone, Olive, or Asphalt color. |
| **Table B (NFC Station)** | Right side — low minimalist table with an iPad (or small stand) showing how to scan NFC chips in the garments. Quietly invites interaction. |
| **Rack B (Hoodies/Coats)** | Right back — heavier garments like hoodies or jackets. Positioned so only serious customers make it deeper into booth. |
| **Banner** | Bone color with small black skull mark + GOOD ENOUGH text, hanging low-key on back of booth (no huge logos). |
| **Traffic Flow** | Customers naturally enter left → browse → NFC scan → deeper purchase decision right side → exit. Creates subtle journey without forcing them. |

⸻

## Design Principles

- **Negative Space = Luxury:**  
  Space between products must feel intentional — **no cluttered racks**.
  
- **Interaction at Their Pace:**  
  NFC scan station is passive — no aggressive sales.  
  Staff acts like docents: "If you want to check the garment, you can tap here."

- **No Shouting:**  
  No banners with "SALE!" or "NEW DROP!" vibes.  
  (In fact, NOT saying anything about scarcity makes it feel *more precious.*)

⸻

## Visual Feeling Target  
Imagine if a **Muji store** and a **Supreme back room** had a baby —  
simple, elevated, local, crafted.

## Bonus if You Expand Later (10x20)

You simply double the booth:  
- Left side = **Product Gallery** (clothing racks, tote display)  
- Right side = **Seating Lounge** (2 minimalist chairs, good enough mini-lookbook, deeper storytelling)  
_(Creates a luxury "hangout" vibe even in a farmers market.)_
