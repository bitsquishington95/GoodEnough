# GOOD ENOUGH™ — Farmers Market Booth Strategy

⸻

## 1. Booth Size & Footprint

| Option | Pros | Cons |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **10x10 booth** (standard) | - Lower cost<br>- Easier weekly setup<br>- Fits in 1–2-person team easily | - Very tight if you want storytelling displays + inventory<br>- Less visual impact |
| **10x20 booth** (premium) | - Serious visual presence<br>- Allows for minimal "gallery" aesthetic with negative space<br>- Can run mini-experiential moments (like NFC scanning demos) | - Higher cost (usually 1.5x–2x the fee)<br>- Needs 2 people to run comfortably |

**Recommendation:**  
→ Start **10x10** for first market pilots.  
→ **Option to upgrade** to 10x20 as you scale successful operators.

⸻

## 2. Booth Visual Concept (Affordable but Luxurious)

GOOD ENOUGH booth = **"Field Museum meets Brutalist Streetwear"** vibe.  
Quiet, serious, built for **craft display**, not flash.

**Core Design DNA:**

| Element | Visual Idea | Cost Range |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Canopy | All-matte black or muted Bone (#E7E0D4) canopy, no top signage | $150–$250 |
| Tables | 1–2 collapsible wood-finish tables (NO plastic folding tables) | $100–$150 each |
| Racks | Matte black minimalist pipe racks (collapsible) | $75–$120 each |
| Ground | Outdoor rug in muted tone (bone or olive), covers the concrete or grass and "frames" the booth | $80–$120 |
| Branding | Small GOOD ENOUGH fabric flag sign (~18x36") — discreet, hangs on table corner | ~$40 |
| NFC Scan Station | 1 mounted iPad Mini (or QR poster) for authenticating products live | $300 refurbished iPad / $25 poster |
| Lighting (optional) | Battery-powered minimalist spotlights | $100–$150 |
| Merch Bags | Simple kraft paper bags with micro skull stamp | $0.50–$0.75 each |

**Mood Visual:**  
_(Imagine this look, minimalism-first)_
- Canopy blends into background.
- Booth feels like a **pop-up gallery**.
- Garments spaced out **with tons of breathing room** (luxury = empty space).
- Skull mark appears **only** on product and small flag — not all over.

⸻

## 3. Booth Staffing Structure

| Setup | Details |
|:---|:---|
| **1 person** | Bare minimum; manageable for light traffic weekdays or slow markets; intense at busy events. |
| **2 people** | Recommended standard — one focuses on customer engagement, one handles sales/stock/NFC checking. |

**Roles if 2-person team:**
- **Person A:** Greeter / Storyteller / NFC authenticator
- **Person B:** Cashier / Stock handler / Booth maintenance

→ You could even create a mini GOOD ENOUGH "**Field Manual**" for how booth staff behave:  
(quiet, not salesy, act like *museum docents* not *pushy retailers*.)

⸻

## 4. Booth Weekly Logistics Plan

| Task | Notes |
|:---|:---|
| Setup time | 45 minutes (2 people) |
| Breakdown time | 30 minutes |
| Weekly Inventory | ~75–100 units (tees, totes, socks) = enough for a full weekend with safety margin |
| Transportation | 1 small SUV or van = all gear fits easily |

**Pro Tip:**  
Standardize EVERYTHING into durable **stackable black crates** for gear. (Luxury brands even use these at Baselworld pop-ups.)

⸻

## Summary

**Start 10x10, two people.**  
- Weekly setups easy.  
- Financial risk low.  
- Luxury feel built from **restraint** and **careful minimalism**, not expensive fixtures.

→ Pilot for 90 days.  
→ Then offer high-performing partners a "graduate to 10x20" option if they prove sales can support it.
